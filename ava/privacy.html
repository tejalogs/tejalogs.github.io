Privacy Policy

Effective: 7 September 2026

Ava is a film-emulation camera app for Android, package com.ava.camera, developed by Tejalogs. This policy covers the Ava Android app and nothing else. It describes what the app does with your data, in enough detail that you can check it against the app itself.

The short version. Ava has no accounts, no servers and no analytics, and it sends nothing off your phone — it does not hold Android's internet permission, so it cannot upload anything. Two things are worth knowing anyway. Every photo and clip is copied into your device's normal photo gallery automatically, so any cloud photo backup you already use will upload that copy the same way it uploads everything else you shoot. And the place name Ava prints under each photo is part of the film stock's fiction, not a reading of where you are.

What Ava sends off your device

Nothing. Ava does not hold the Android INTERNET permission, so the app's own process cannot open a network connection. There are no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no advertising network, and no Firebase.

Two honest footnotes to that. Ava does hold ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, a permission added by one of the Android camera libraries it is built on; Google Play will show it in the app's permission list. The image library that displays your diary uses it to check whether the device is online. It reads a yes-or-no answer and sends nothing, and without the internet permission it could not send anything if it tried. Separately, tapping the privacy link inside the app opens this page in your browser — your browser then makes that request, not Ava.

What Ava stores on your device

Ava keeps data in four places inside its own private storage, plus your gallery.

  • Your photos and clips — the copies your diary shows, as ordinary JPEG and MP4 files.
  • A small database — one row per photo or clip, holding the file path, the film stock's name, the time it was saved, any caption you typed, the film stock's place label, the exposure readout, and a reference to the gallery copy.
  • Three settings — your current film stock, its strength, and whether you have finished the intro. Nothing else.
  • Temporary capture files — while a photo is being developed or a clip is being recorded, the work-in-progress file sits in Ava's cache and is deleted immediately afterwards. If a capture is interrupted, a file can be left behind there; clearing the app's cache or uninstalling removes it.

Captions are the only thing here you type; everything else in that list is your photographs, which are personal in themselves. Ava never writes a caption for you, and nothing analyses one or sends it anywhere on its own. A caption is shown in your diary and included as text when you share.

The copy in your gallery

Every photo and clip Ava keeps is also copied into your device's shared photo library, automatically, whenever that write succeeds. There is no setting for this and no way to turn it off.

On Android 10 and later the copy goes into a folder named Ava — under Pictures for photos, Movies for clips. On Android 9 and earlier Ava does not set a folder, so the copy lands wherever your device files it. Each copy is named for the app and the moment it was taken — Ava_ followed by the capture time in milliseconds.

That copy is an ordinary picture in your photo library. Any app you have given access to your media can see it, including its filename. If you have a cloud photo backup switched on, that service will back it up like any other photo you take. Ava itself uploads nothing — but this is the point where your photos become reachable by software other than Ava, so it is the most important sentence on this page.

The copy is best-effort. If your device refuses the write — most often on Android 9 and earlier, when the storage permission below has been declined — Ava keeps the private copy and continues without showing you an error. In that case the photo exists in Ava's diary and not in your gallery.

About the microphone

Ava asks for the microphone the moment you switch to CINE mode, before you record anything — that is earlier than you might expect, and you may see the request even if the camera itself is unavailable. It asks a second time if you tap record while the microphone is still ungranted. Two refusals is Android's threshold for hiding a request permanently; after that CINE keeps working and records silent. The permission is used in exactly one place: attaching sound to a clip as it starts recording. There is no background listening and no other audio capture anywhere in the app.

Three things worth knowing. Tapping record before you answer the request starts a silent clip rather than no clip. Once you have granted the microphone, every CINE clip has sound — there is no per-clip mute. And clips stop automatically at about fifteen seconds. Recorded audio travels inside the clip, including into the gallery copy described above. If you decline the microphone, CINE still works and records silent.

Location, and the place names on your photos

Ava never accesses your location. It holds no location permission, calls no location or geocoding service, and writes no GPS tag into any photo or clip.

The place name shown under a photo in the diary — VARANASI, DELHI, ROOFTOP — is a fixed label belonging to the film stock you shot with. It is part of the app's fiction. It is the same for everyone using that stock, it is not derived from your device, and it is stored only in Ava's own database. It is never written into the image file and never included when you share.

What Ava writes into your photos

A photo Ava saves is re-encoded from the image itself, so it does not inherit the metadata your phone's camera recorded. In its place Ava stamps an invented camera identity: a make of AVA, a model of CR-206, and its own software name. Where the on-screen readout can be parsed it also writes up to eight exposure figures — aperture as both an f-number and an APEX value, sensitivity, exposure time, exposure compensation, focal length both actual and 35mm-equivalent, and a lens name — some of which are characteristics of the film stock rather than measurements of the scene.

No location tag and no date or time tag is written. Clips are not stamped at all; they carry only what your device's video recorder writes into them, which does not include location.

Sharing

When you share a photo or clip, Ava hands the app you choose a read-only handle to that one file, plus your caption if you wrote one. Every share also appends the text #AvaCamera, whether or not you wrote a caption. Once the file reaches the app you picked, that app's own privacy policy governs what happens to it. Ava has no part in it and no way to reach it afterwards.

No other app can reach Ava's private storage. A share grants access to the single file you selected and to nothing else — not to your other photos, and not to Ava's database.

Keeping and deleting your data

Ava keeps what you make indefinitely, until you delete it. There is no expiry, no automatic clean-up and no account to close. The one exception is installing an older version of Ava than the one you have: the diary index is rebuilt from scratch and your existing entries stop appearing, though the image files stay in Ava's storage until you uninstall.

Deleting a memory deletes Ava's own copy and the copy in your gallery, and then removes the entry from your diary. If Ava cannot delete its own copy, the entry stays in your diary and Ava tells you so — nothing is stranded somewhere you can no longer see it. If it is the gallery copy that will not go, the entry is removed and Ava tells you to delete the leftover in your gallery app. There is no delete-everything button; memories are deleted one at a time.

Uninstalling Ava, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes everything in the four private locations listed above — your diary, its database and its settings. It does not remove the copies in your gallery, which are ordinary photos in your library and remain after the app is gone.

How your data is protected

  • Ava's own diary, database and settings live in its private app storage, which Android isolates from other apps. The gallery copy described above is deliberately outside that sandbox.
  • Ava adds no encryption of its own. Your photos and diary are protected by Android's app sandbox and by whatever device encryption your phone provides. On most modern devices that means Ava's files are encrypted until you first unlock the phone after a restart.
  • The component that hands files to other apps is not exported and releases one file at a time, only to the app you pick, and only for reading. Ava's database is outside its reach.
  • Your photos, your diary and your settings are excluded from Google's cloud backup and from Android's phone-to-phone transfer. Both are declared explicitly by the app rather than left to a default, so setting up a new handset does not carry your Ava diary across to it.
  • Ava does not shield its screens from screenshots or screen recording, so the viewfinder and your diary appear in the app switcher and can be captured like any other app.
  • There is no transmission to protect: the app cannot reach a network.

Information about your device

To decide how large a photo it can process, Ava reads how much memory your phone has and how large an image its graphics hardware can handle. It also reads your system animation setting so it can respect "remove animations". These are read while the app runs, are used only for that purpose and are not stored. The camera library Ava is built on also checks your phone's make and model against its own list of known hardware quirks. None of these are identifiers: Ava reads no device ID, no advertising ID, and no hardware serial number of any kind.

Your rights

Data-protection law gives you rights over personal data a company holds about you — to see it, correct it, take a copy, have it erased, or object to its use.

The developer of Ava never receives any of your data, so there is nothing held to disclose, correct, export or erase. In practice you exercise these rights directly on your phone: your photos and captions are files and a database on your own device, you can read and change them in the app, you can copy them out through your gallery or the share sheet, and you can erase them by deleting a memory or uninstalling the app. Ava does not sell or share personal information, and there is no profiling, no automated decision-making and no advertising. If you are in a region with a data-protection authority, you retain the right to complain to it.

Children

Ava is a general-audience camera app. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone — there is nothing for the developer to collect. If you are below the age of digital consent where you live, please use Ava with a parent or guardian.
