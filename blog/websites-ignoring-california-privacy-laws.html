Widespread Non-Compliance Undermines California's Privacy Laws

By Teja (tejalogs) · 2026-06-09
The short answerA recent survey by The Markup found that numerous prominent websites are violating California's privacy laws, including the CCPA and CPRA, at an 'industrial scale'. This widespread non-compliance signals a fundamental breakdown in user data protection due to weak enforcement rather than a lack of regulatory frameworks.
Key facts
  • The Markup survey found 'industrial scale' non-compliance with California privacy laws.
  • Websites are widely violating the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA).
  • Weak enforcement is enabling systemic disregard for user data protection.
  • Privacy is shifting from a legal checkbox to a core component of user experience.
  • Businesses prioritizing privacy-first design will gain a competitive edge.

California Privacy Laws Ignored at 'Industrial Scale'

The Markup's recent investigation revealed that a significant number of prominent websites are failing to adhere to California's privacy regulations. This non-compliance with laws like the CCPA and CPRA is not isolated, but occurs at an 'industrial scale', indicating a systemic disregard for user data rights across the digital landscape. Millions of Californian users are impacted by these pervasive violations.

Enforcement Gaps Enable Systemic Disregard

The widespread failure of websites to comply with the CCPA and CPRA suggests that regulatory frameworks alone are insufficient without robust enforcement. The 'industrial scale' of non-compliance implies that current oversight mechanisms are not deterring companies from flouting user data protection. This situation exposes a critical gap where laws exist but their practical application for users remains compromised.

Privacy Shifts from Compliance to Core UX

For businesses, this trend signals a critical shift: privacy is evolving from a legal checkbox to a fundamental component of user experience and brand reputation. Companies that prioritize ethical data handling and user transparency, moving beyond minimum legal requirements, will differentiate themselves. Proactive privacy-first design will become crucial for maintaining user trust and avoiding future regulatory backlash in an increasingly scrutinized digital environment.

FAQ

What California privacy laws are websites violating?

Websites are widely violating California's privacy laws, specifically the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA).

What does 'industrial scale' non-compliance mean for users?

'Industrial scale' non-compliance means a vast number of online platforms are failing to protect user data, impacting potentially millions of Californian users by disregarding their privacy rights under CCPA and CPRA.
